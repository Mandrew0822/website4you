How long does it take to build a website?

Short answer: a focused one-page site takes about a week, a small business site one to two weeks, and a larger agency project one to three months. The longer answer is that content and decisions matter more than code.

The short answer

For a small custom website, plan for one to three weeks from the first real conversation to launch. A one-page site can be live in about five to seven days. A five-page business site typically takes ten to fifteen working days.

Those numbers assume two things: the scope is agreed, and content decisions happen quickly. Most projects that run long are not slowed down by design or development. They are waiting on text, photos, or feedback.

Timelines by project type

Project Typical timeline Main variable
One-page landing page 3–7 working days Copy and offer clarity
One-page portfolio 5–7 working days Image selection and project notes
Small business site (up to 5 pages) 10–15 working days Page content and feedback
Website redesign 2–3 weeks Audit findings and redirects
Agency brand + website project 6–12 weeks Stakeholders, strategy, and approvals
E-commerce or custom application 4–12+ weeks Features, integrations, and testing

What happens at each stage

1. Brief and scope (day 1–2)

You tell us about the business, the audience, and the goal. We reply with questions, a proposed page list, a fixed price, and a timeline. Nothing starts until the scope is agreed in writing.

2. Content and direction (day 2–4)

You send text, images, and any brand assets. We shape the structure, write or polish copy where needed, and agree the visual direction. This is the stage where a project can quietly stall, so it helps to have a decision-maker available.

3. Design (day 3–6)

You see a first look at the key page, usually the homepage. We refine the layout, type, colour, and hierarchy together. Approving this direction early prevents expensive changes later.

4. Build (day 5–9)

The approved design becomes a responsive site. We build the pages, connect forms or booking links, add metadata and structured data, and test across screen sizes.

5. Revisions (day 8–11)

You review the full site and send one consolidated round of feedback. We make the changes, check the details, and prepare for launch.

6. Launch (day 10–12)

We connect the domain, check forms and analytics, submit the sitemap, and hand over the files and accounts. You own everything.

What slows a project down

  • Missing content. Placeholder text hides layout problems and delays the real review.
  • Slow feedback. A three-day pause at each stage can add a week or more.
  • Too many decision-makers. Five opinions without one owner creates loops.
  • Scope creep. “While we’re here…” additions need time and often money.
  • Integrations with unknown requirements. Booking systems and CRMs need testing, not guessing.
  • Late image sourcing. Good photography takes longer than people expect.
  • Unclear approval. If nobody can say “yes, that’s it,” the project keeps moving without landing.

How to speed things up

  1. Write your content first. Even rough notes are enough to start. Waiting until the site is designed is the slowest possible order.
  2. Pick one decision-maker. Gather opinions, but give one person the final call.
  3. Consolidate feedback. One clear list beats twelve messages spread over a week.
  4. Approve the direction early. Fixing a layout after the build starts takes longer than fixing it at the design stage.
  5. Choose a focused scope. A strong five-page site beats a mediocre ten-page site, and it launches sooner.
Rule of thumb

Assume the build will take the time we say. Then add however long you need to write the content and reply to feedback. That is your real timeline.

Redesign timelines

A redesign adds two things to a normal build: an audit of the existing site and a migration plan. The audit covers content, traffic, mobile experience, speed, and basic SEO. The migration plan covers URL changes, redirects, and what must stay intact to protect existing visibility.

Most small business redesigns take two to three weeks. Larger sites with many pages or a store take longer. The design work itself is often similar to a new site — the extra care goes into what you keep.

Questions, answered

Can a website be built in a week?

Yes, a focused one-page site can be designed, built, and launched in about a week when the content and feedback are ready. Multi-page sites usually need one to two weeks, and larger projects need longer.

What is the biggest cause of delays?

Waiting on content and feedback. Design and development are usually predictable; the variable is how quickly decisions are made, copy is supplied, and revisions are approved.

How long does a redesign take?

A redesign adds an audit and migration planning to the normal build. Most small business redesigns take two to three weeks, assuming content decisions are made promptly and redirects are straightforward.

Do revisions reset the timeline?

They add time, but they do not reset the project. Each revision round is scheduled and focused so we can keep momentum. Consolidating feedback into one round is the fastest approach.
